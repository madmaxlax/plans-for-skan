## 🏖️ Beaches & Water

### Clift Park Beach — Skaneateles Lake ⭐
- **Address:** 15 W Genesee St, Skaneateles, NY
- **Season:** Late June – late August (lifeguards on duty **11am–7pm**, 7 days/week)
- **What to know:** Famously shallow water — kids can walk far out and stay safe. Crystal-clear, Caribbean-blue water. Staff sprays off everyone's feet before entry. Gazebo, stone seating, and lawn. **Free public access.**
- **Note for June:** Lifeguards may not start until end of June. Check with Town of Skaneateles before planning a swim day.

### Owasco Splash Pad (Town of Owasco) ⭐ — Perfect for Egan
- **Address:** 2 Bristol Ave, Auburn, NY 13021 (~15 min from Skaneateles)
- **Season:** Opens **mid-June** (weather-dependent)
- **Hours:** 9am–8pm, all days
- **Cost:** FREE
- **What to know:** Water sprays, fountains, interactive features. Adjacent playground and bathrooms on site. Locals say it's ideal for toddlers — flat, no pool depth, fully enclosed spray area. Highly recommend as a daily-easy outing.

### Emerson Park — Owasco Lake
- **Address:** East Lake Rd, Auburn, NY (at the tip of Owasco Lake) (~20 min)
- **Hours:** Dawn to dusk, year-round
- **What to know:** Beach area, multiple playgrounds, merry-go-round, pavilion, disc golf, kayak/canoe rentals. Great for a full-day family outing. A $25M upgrade is currently underway — new island playground, amphitheater coming.
- **Tip:** Pack a picnic; pavilion shelter can be reserved.

### Casey Park Pool (Auburn)
- **Operated by:** Auburn YMCA
- **Hours:** Noon–6pm (summer); seasonal outdoor pool
- **Cost:** YMCA pricing; call ahead
- **Good for:** Older kids primarily, but worth a visit on hot days

## 🌱 At-Home Nature & Science Activities

> Things to do *at the lake house* with Egan (turning 2 in June). No drive required — just the yard, the dock, the woods, and a toddler with good boots. All activities are safe for 2-year-olds with normal supervision.

---

### 🌿 Planting & Growing

#### 1. Sprout a Radish (or Bean) from Seed
**What you do:** Press a radish or bean seed into a small pot of dirt, water it, and check on it every morning. Radishes sprout in 3–5 days — fast enough to keep a toddler's attention.
**You need:** Small pots or plastic cups, potting soil, radish or bean seeds
**Toddler note:** Let Egan do the digging and watering himself. The payoff is fast enough that he'll remember planting it.

#### 2. Grass Head Planter
**What you do:** Fill a knee-high stocking or cup with soil and grass seed, tie it into a head shape, draw on a face with a marker, and water it. Watch "hair" grow in about a week.
**You need:** Old nylon stocking or plastic cup, grass seed, soil, googly eyes or marker
**Toddler note:** Great for drawing the face — giant silly smile and googly eyes are mandatory.

#### 3. Press Wildflowers & Leaves
**What you do:** Gather flowers, leaves, and ferns from around the yard or woods edge. Press them flat between sheets of wax paper inside a heavy book for 5–7 days. Pull them out and tape onto paper to make nature art.
**You need:** Wax paper, heavy books, collected plants, tape or glue stick
**Toddler note:** The foraging part — walking around choosing which leaves to pick — is the whole activity for a 2-year-old.

#### 4. Seed Ball Toss
**What you do:** Mix wildflower seeds into a ball of damp clay or mud. Let Egan squish the mixture, roll it into balls, and then toss them into the yard. Water them over the next few days and see what blooms.
**You need:** Wildflower seed mix, air-dry clay or just dirt/mud, water
**Toddler note:** Requires zero precision and involves throwing things — basically perfect.

---

### 🐛 Bugs & Creatures

#### 5. Bug Hotel
**What you do:** Stack up a collection of hollow sticks, pinecones, bark scraps, rolled cardboard, and leaves inside a small open wooden box (or just a big tin can). Lay it at the edge of the woods. Check it for visitors each day.
**You need:** A tin can or small box, sticks, pinecones, bark, dead leaves, twine to hang (optional)
**Toddler note:** Make it together — Egan picks the sticks, you stuff them in. Then do a daily "bug check." Don't expect immediate results; the mystery of waiting is part of it.

#### 6. Hunt for Roly-Polies (Pill Bugs)
**What you do:** Lift rocks, logs, and bark near damp soil around the yard or woods edge. Find roly-polies (pill bugs), watch them curl up, count them, and then put them back.
**You need:** Nothing — just sneakers and a sense of adventure
**Toddler note:** Roly-polies are the best bug for toddlers — they're harmless, slow, and they *roll into a ball*. Expect Egan to want to do this every morning.

#### 7. Bug Observation Jar
**What you do:** Catch a grasshopper, beetle, or caterpillar in a jar with a few leaves and a stick. Observe for 10–15 minutes, then release it right where you found it.
**You need:** Clean mason jar with a few small holes poked in the lid, magnifying glass
**Toddler note:** Keep it short — catch, look, let it go. Make releasing the bug as ceremonial as catching it.

#### 8. Firefly Watch (Evening Activity)
**What you do:** After dinner, go sit in the grass or on the dock as it gets dark. Watch for fireflies. Try to catch one gently in cupped hands, look at it, and let it go.
**You need:** Nothing
**Toddler note:** June evenings in Upstate NY are peak firefly season. Egan watching fireflies blink over a lake for the first time is a core memory.

### 💧 Water Science

#### 9. Sink or Float Experiments
**What you do:** Fill a bucket or bin with lake water. Collect objects from around the yard — pebbles, sticks, leaves, a bottle cap, a feather, a pinecone — and test each one: does it sink or float? Make predictions before each drop.
**You need:** Big bin or bucket, water, random objects from nature
**Toddler note:** "Will it sink or float? Let's find out!" is the whole script. Egan will want to do this 40 times in a row, which is fine.

#### 10. Rain Gauge & Puddle Science
**What you do:** After a June rain, mark puddle edges with sticks or chalk. Come back in a few hours — did it get bigger or smaller? Where did the water go? Also make a simple rain gauge from a clear plastic cup and a ruler — tape on the outside.
**You need:** Sticks or sidewalk chalk, clear plastic cup, ruler or tape measure
**Toddler note:** Pairs well with jumping in the puddles. Science first, then splash.

#### 11. Water Pouring Station
**What you do:** Set up an outdoor sensory bin with cups, funnels, small pitchers, and containers of different sizes. Let Egan pour, scoop, splash, and transfer water for as long as he wants. Add a few pebbles or leaves for texture.
**You need:** Plastic tub, assorted cups and containers, funnel, measuring cups
**Toddler note:** This is the highest ROI activity on this list. Occupies a 2-year-old for 45+ minutes. Put it near a hose so you can refill.

#### 12. Mud Kitchen
**What you do:** Give Egan a patch of dirt, some water, old cups and spoons, and declare it a kitchen. He makes "soup," "pie," whatever. No script needed.
**You need:** Dirt patch, water source, old spoons/cups/muffin tin
**Toddler note:** Sensory play gold. Bring a change of clothes.

---

### 🪨 Rocks & Earth

#### 13. Rock Painting & Rock Hunt
**What you do:** Collect smooth rocks from the lakefront or yard. Rinse and dry them, then paint faces, animals, or patterns with washable paint. Display on a windowsill or hide them around the yard.
**You need:** Smooth rocks, washable tempera paint, foam paintbrushes
**Toddler note:** Egan can dab paint onto rocks without any instruction. The "hiding them for others to find" step is fun and gives the activity legs.

#### 14. Dirt Layer Jar
**What you do:** Fill a clear jar with layers of different things you find outside — coarse sand, fine soil, small pebbles, leaves. Add water and shake it up. Watch the layers settle over the next hour.
**You need:** Clear mason jar, soil, sand, small pebbles, leaves, water
**Toddler note:** The shaking is the best part. The settling and layer formation is genuinely interesting to watch — even for adults.

#### 15. Fossil & Print Hunt
**What you do:** Press leaves, shells, or sticks into flattened air-dry clay to make impressions. Let dry. You get a homemade "fossil."
**You need:** Air-dry clay (or salt dough), leaves, small shells, sticks
**Toddler note:** Let Egan pick which leaf to press. He can do the pressing himself with help. Nice keepsake if you make it with his handprint too.

---

### 🐦 Birds & Sky

#### 16. Homemade Bird Feeder
**What you do:** Roll a toilet paper roll in peanut butter, then roll it in birdseed. Hang it on a branch near a window and watch birds come.
**You need:** Toilet paper roll, peanut butter (or sunflower butter), birdseed, string
**Toddler note:** Egan can do the rolling step. Then watch together from the window with a cup of morning coffee/milk.

#### 17. Cloud Watching Journal
**What you do:** Lie in the grass with Egan and look at clouds. Take photos on your phone. At the end of the day, print (or just save) the photos and write down what he said the clouds looked like — his words, not yours.
**You need:** Your phone; optional: a small blank notebook
**Toddler note:** The point is his imagination, not meteorology. "That one is a dog. That one is a truck. That one is Grandma." Keep a running log — it's a great trip artifact.

#### 18. Make a Bird Sound Walk
**What you do:** Walk the yard or the woods edge slowly and quietly in the morning. Every time you hear a bird, stop and listen. Try to find it. Name it if you can (Merlin app from Cornell Lab is free and can ID birds by sound).
**You need:** Nothing — Merlin app recommended (free, iOS/Android)
**Toddler note:** Doesn't require seeing a bird. "Did you hear that? That was a chickadee." Builds attention and listening skills.

---

### 🔬 Simple Experiments

#### 19. Baking Soda + Vinegar Volcano
**What you do:** Shape a small mound of dirt or playdough around a cup. Put a spoonful of baking soda in the cup. Pour in a splash of white vinegar. Watch it fizz and overflow.
**You need:** Baking soda, white vinegar, cup or small container, dirt or playdough
**Toddler note:** Will ask to do this 20 times in a row. Buy a big box of baking soda. You've been warned.

#### 20. Magnifying Glass Exploration
**What you do:** Take a magnifying glass outside and look at anything — bark, a leaf, an ant, moss, the ground, a flower petal. Narrate what you see. Egan looks, you describe it.
**You need:** Cheap plastic magnifying glass (the bigger the better for toddler hands)
**Toddler note:** The magic of everything looking 5x bigger never gets old. Put it in your pocket every time you go outside.
